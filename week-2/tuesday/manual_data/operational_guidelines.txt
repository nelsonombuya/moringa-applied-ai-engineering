AFYAPLUS HEALTH APP — SYNTHETIC OPERATIONAL GUIDELINES
Version: 1.0 | Effective date: 2025-01-15

Purpose
Provide consistent, safe, and privacy-conscious guidance for routine hospital and health-app operations. This document is synthetic training data and must not be used as clinical advice or as a substitute for approved hospital policy.

Service hours and response targets
- Emergency support: 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. If a user reports severe symptoms or immediate danger, advise them to contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department.
- Nurse triage queue: 07:00–19:00 daily. Target first response: within 30 minutes.
- Routine appointment requests: confirmation within 1 business day.
- Prescription refill requests: review within 2 business days; controlled medicines require clinician review.
- Technical support: Monday–Friday, 08:00–17:00. Target first response: within 4 business hours.

Appointment operations
- Verify patient identity using at least two approved identifiers before discussing protected health information.
- Confirm appointment type, clinician, location or telehealth link, date, time zone, and preparation instructions.
- Patients may cancel or reschedule through the app up to 24 hours before the appointment where permitted by local policy.
- Mark a patient as a no-show only after the configured grace period has elapsed and contact attempts are documented.
- Do not diagnose, prescribe, or change treatment through automated chat.

Patient communications
- Use clear, respectful, nonjudgmental language and confirm understanding when instructions are important.
- Share only the minimum necessary information. Do not display full medical details in push notifications.
- Never request passwords, one-time authentication codes, or complete payment-card numbers in chat.
- Route complaints, safeguarding concerns, suspected abuse, and service incidents to the designated human team.

Escalation rules
- Immediate escalation: chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, stroke symptoms, uncontrolled bleeding, loss of consciousness, imminent self-harm risk, or other life-threatening symptoms.
- Urgent clinical review: rapidly worsening symptoms, high-risk pregnancy concerns, serious medication reactions, or significant abnormal results flagged by a clinician.
- Privacy or security incident: stop sharing data, preserve the relevant record, and notify the privacy/security team according to incident procedure.
- When uncertain, acknowledge the limitation and route the case to a qualified human professional.

Data quality and records
- Record the date, time, source, action taken, responsible team, and follow-up status for operational events.
- Use standardized statuses: New, In review, Awaiting patient, Escalated, Resolved, and Closed.
- Correct errors using an auditable amendment; do not delete or overwrite clinical records without authorization.
- Retain and disclose information according to applicable law, consent settings, and organizational retention policy.
